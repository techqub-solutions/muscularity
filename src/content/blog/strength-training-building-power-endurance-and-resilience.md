Strength training, also called resistance training, is one of the most effective ways to build muscle, boost your metabolism and make your body more resilient. Weights, resistance bands or your own bodyweight all work.

## What is strength training?

Strength training makes your muscles work against resistance: free weights, machines, bands or bodyweight. The goal is to build stronger, bigger muscles, better endurance and a more stable body.

## Why strength training matters

- **More muscle and definition:** stronger, bigger muscle fibres and a toned look.
- **A faster metabolism:** muscle burns more calories than fat, even at rest.
- **Strength and endurance:** carrying shopping, climbing stairs and playing sport all get easier.
- **Stronger bones:** loading your bones makes them denser, which helps prevent osteoporosis as you age.
- **Injury prevention:** strong muscles support and stabilise your joints.
- **Better mental health:** training releases endorphins, and getting stronger builds confidence.

## Types of strength training

- **Free weights:** dumbbells and barbells. Squats, deadlifts and bench presses build strength, balance and coordination.
- **Bodyweight exercises:** push-ups, pull-ups, squats and lunges. No equipment needed.
- **Machines and cables:** great for beginners and for targeting specific muscles.
- **Resistance bands:** low-impact and perfect for training at home.
- **Plyometrics:** jump squats, box jumps and burpees for power and speed.

## How often should you train?

Two to four sessions a week gives good results. You can split your sessions by muscle group (upper body one day, lower body the next) or train your full body several times a week.

## Recovery is where you grow

Training creates tiny tears in your muscle fibres, and they grow back stronger while you recover. Rest, nutrition (especially protein) and hydration are all essential.
